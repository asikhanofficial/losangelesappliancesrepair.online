In-Home Gas & Electric Dryer Repair

When a clothes dryer stops generating heat or fails to rotate, damp laundry accumulates fast. Clothes dryers combine high-current electrical resistance or natural gas combustion with heavy centrifugal airflow and thermal cut-off switches.

Because clothes dryers involve heat generation and venting, proper diagnostic care is vital not just for drying convenience, but for household fire safety and energy efficiency.

Common Dryer Symptoms We Resolve

  • No Heat Produced: The drum rotates and air blows through the vent, but clothes remain cold and wet after a full cycle.
  • Drum Won't Turn: The motor hums or starts, but the heavy inner drum does not rotate due to a snapped belt or worn idler pulley.
  • Loud Squealing or Thumping: High-pitched metal screeching caused by dried drum support roller bearings or worn front glides.
  • Dryer Overheating & Shutting Off: The exterior cabinet feels scalding to the touch and the unit turns off after 10 minutes due to restricted internal airflow.
  • Will Not Start at All: Complete lack of response when pressing start, often traced to a blown thermal fuse or broken door latch.

Our Diagnostic Process

Our technicians systematically isolate electrical and combustion circuits:

  • Thermal Circuit Testing: Testing continuity across the thermal fuse, high-limit thermostat, and cycling thermostat with a digital multimeter.
  • Heating Source Check: In electric units, testing resistance across the coiled heating element. In gas units, testing radiant flame sensors, hot surface igniters, and gas solenoid coils.
  • Airflow & Blower Wheel Audit: Inspecting the squirrel-cage blower fan for lint clogging or stripping on the motor drive shaft.
  • Mechanical Support Check: Checking roller axle lubrication, idler arm tension, and drum belt wear.

Common Dryer Parts Repaired or Replaced

  • Heating Elements & Gas Burner Igniters: Generating controlled drying heat inside the airflow duct.
  • Thermal Fuses & Limit Thermostats: Safety devices that cut power if internal temperatures reach unsafe levels.
  • Drum Drive Belts & Idler Pulleys: Keeping continuous tension on the drum perimeter for smooth spinning.
  • Drum Support Rollers & Axles: Bearing the heavy weight of wet laundry during high-speed rotation.
  • Door Switches & Timers: Ensuring safe door closure and cycle timing accuracy.

Why Professional Repair Makes Sense

Replacing a dryer often involves re-routing vent ducts, adjusting electrical 240V plugs, or running new gas flex lines. Professional on-site repair resolves the specific failed heating coil, sensor, or belt in a single visit, saving hundreds of dollars while preserving your matched laundry pair.
