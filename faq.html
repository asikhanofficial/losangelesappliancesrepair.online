Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers regarding home appliance diagnostics, scheduling, and repair options in Los Angeles.

We service all major residential kitchen and laundry appliances. This includes refrigerators, freezers, dishwashers, washing machines, clothes dryers, built-in ovens, cooktops, stoves, and freestanding ranges. Both standard household configurations and higher-end residential units are supported.

A general guideline is the 50% rule: if the appliance has surpassed half of its expected operating lifespan (typically 10–15 years for most major units) and the cost of repair approaches 50% of the price of a brand-new comparable replacement, replacement may be worth considering. However, many common issues—such as a worn drain pump, faulty heating element, or blown thermal fuse—are economical repairs that restore years of reliable life. Our technicians provide an honest breakdown during the visit.

Clear a direct pathway to the unit and remove any clutter from around the appliance. If your refrigerator or freezer is malfunctioning, try to keep the doors closed to conserve internal temperatures. For dishwashers or washing machines, please remove standing laundry or dishes if practical. Locating the model number tag in advance is also helpful.

The technician arrives at your home, listens to your description of the symptoms, and performs a thorough mechanical and electronic evaluation. This includes checking voltage, testing motor windings, inspecting water seals, examining control modules, and identifying defective components. The technician then explains the diagnostic findings and outlines the recommended repair procedure.

If the appliance exhibits water leaks, burning odors, sparking, or unusual electrical trips on your circuit breaker, it is strongly recommended to discontinue use and disconnect the power supply until a technician completes an evaluation. Operating a compromised appliance can worsen component wear or pose household risks.

Yes. Many Los Angeles residences feature built-in wall ovens, drop-in cooktops, and custom panel-ready refrigerators or dishwashers. Our service takes careful precautions around custom cabinetry and stone countertops during removal and reinstallation.

Model numbers are typically printed on a metal or silver label. For refrigerators, check the upper interior wall or inside the door jamb. For dishwashers, check the metal rim of the inner tub or door edge. For washing machines and dryers, look inside the door rim or behind the lower access panel. For ovens and ranges, look around the storage drawer rim or along the oven door opening.
